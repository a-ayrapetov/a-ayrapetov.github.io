IT to OT, level by level

The Purdue model from the enterprise down to the field. Open a level to see what I do there and the tools I publish for it.

Levels 4 and 5: Enterprise IT identity, campus, cloud
  • Cisco ISE design, upgrades and rescue; 802.1X EAP-TLS, TrustSec, PKI
  • SD-Access and Catalyst Center; Secure Firewall; SD-WAN and BGP to the cloud
Tools: ise-endpoint-explain, ise-health-audit, cisco-interface-health
Conduit: firewall and jump host. Nothing from IT reaches operations directly.
Level 3.5: OT DMZ the only way between IT and OT
  • Jump hosts, patch relay, historian replica
  • Vendor remote access through a jump host with MFA
  • Every conduit modelled and checked against real traffic
Tools: ot-zone-conduit-check
Conduit: firewall and monitoring, only the services the conduit allows.
Level 3: Operations control center
  • Security groups across train control, SCADA, fire alarm, elevators, fare collection and CCTV, mapped to IEC 62443
  • NDR sensors and flow analytics, tuned until the alarms mean something
Tools: ndr-coverage-map, cisco-config-drift
Security groups and closed-mode MAB between operations and the stations.
Level 2: Supervisory stations and HMIs
  • Closed-mode MAB for devices that cannot run 802.1X, one site at a time
  • AAA hardening on thousands of switches, checked before it can lock anyone out
  • Discovery of devices nobody had on a list
Tools: ot-asset-inventory, aaa-lockout-check, ios-hardening-audit
One way out through a data diode. Nothing comes back in.
Levels 1 and 0: Control and field train control, PLCs, signals
  • Safety systems stay agentless
  • Visibility through passive TAPs cut in during one overnight window, no service disruption
Tools: ndr-coverage-map
